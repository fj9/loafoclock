| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Aliquot jar | A small straight-sided jar holding a sample of the dough, marked at its starting level, used to judge how much the whole dough has risen |
| Autolyse | Resting flour and water before adding starter and salt |
| Baker's percentage | Each ingredient as a percentage of flour weight |
| Banneton | Basket for the final proof |
| Batard | Oval-shaped loaf |
| Bench rest | A short rest between pre-shaping and final shaping that lets the dough relax |
| Boule | Round-shaped loaf |
| Bulk fermentation | The first rise of the whole dough mass |
| Cold retard | Another name for a fridge proof |
| Couche | A heavy linen cloth that supports the dough while it proofs |
| Crumb | The interior texture of the bread |
| DDT | Desired dough temperature |
| Discard | The portion of starter removed when feeding |
| Dutch oven | A lidded cast-iron pot used to bake the loaf; it traps steam |
| Ear | The raised flap of crust along the score |
| Feeding | Adding fresh flour and water to the starter |
| Fermentation | The process in which the yeast and bacteria feed on the flour, making gas and flavour |
| Fermentolyse | Mixing the active starter into the rested flour and water and resting again, before the salt goes in |
| Fridge proof | The final proof in the fridge, which slows fermentation and builds flavour |
| Gluten | The protein in wheat flour that makes dough stretchy and holds the gas |
| Hooch | Liquid on a hungry starter's surface |
| Hydration | Water as a percentage of flour weight |
| Inclusions | Extras mixed into the dough, such as nuts, dried fruit, olives or cheese |
| Lame | Blade used to score bread |
| Laminating fold | A fold where you stretch the dough thin, scatter the inclusions over it and fold it up so they spread evenly |
| Leaven | Another name for a starter, or for the portion of it used in a bake (see Levain) |
| Levain | Not a different thing from a starter: a portion of it built up in a separate jar for a specific bake |
| Oven spring | The final rise in the oven |
| Overfermented | Bulk fermentation that has gone on too long: the dough is slack and sticky, and smells sharply sour |
| Overproofed | A final proof that has gone on too long: the dough stays dented when poked |
| Peak | When a fed starter has risen to its maximum |
| Poke test | Pressing the dough to judge proof |
| Pre-shape | Loose first shaping before the bench rest |
| Proof | The final rise after shaping |
| Retard | See fridge proof |
| Ripe | A starter that has risen to about double with a domed top, and is ready to use; the peak is its highest point |
| Score | To cut the top of the loaf with a blade so it opens where you want it to |
| Starter | The living culture of wild yeast and bacteria that is kept in flour and water and makes the bread rise. Also called a leaven or sourdough culture; see Levain |
| Stretch and fold | A gentle method for building dough strength |
| Strong flour | Bread flour with a high protein content (about 12 to 14%), which makes strong gluten |
| Underfermented | Bulk fermentation that stopped too early: the dough is dense and tears when shaped |
| Underproofed | A final proof that stopped too early: the dough springs back quickly when poked |
| Windowpane test | Stretching dough thin to see if gluten is developed |

UK and US names for ingredients and kit are in the [[appendix|appendix]].
