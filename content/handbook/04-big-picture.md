Sourdough is bread leavened by a **starter**: a living culture of wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria (LAB) kept in flour and water. No packet yeast is needed.

- **Wild yeast** produces carbon dioxide, which inflates the dough.
- **Lactic acid bacteria** produce lactic and acetic acids, which give the tang, improve keeping quality and help the dough's structure.
- **Time and temperature** control how fast all of this happens. This is the whole craft: warm and fast means milder and quicker; cool and slow means more flavour and more flexibility.

The stages in order:

- Keep a healthy starter
- Build or refresh it so it is at its peak
- Mix flour, water, starter (and salt)
- Develop strength (folds or kneading)
- Bulk fermentation (the first rise)
- Divide, pre-shape, rest
- Final shape
- Proof (the second rise), at room temperature or in the fridge
- Score and bake with steam
- Cool before cutting
