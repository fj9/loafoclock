## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Notes |
|---|---|
| Flour | Strong/bread flour (roughly 12–14% protein) gives the best structure. Add wholemeal, rye or spelt for flavour and a livelier ferment. |
| Water | Tap water is fine for almost everyone. Lukewarm, around 25–30°C, unless you are targeting a specific dough temperature. |
| Salt | About 2% of flour weight. Fine sea salt dissolves most easily. Salt flavours the bread and tightens the dough. |
| Starter | Active and recently fed (see [[starter|section 3]]). |
| Honey | Optional. A small amount, part of the app's recipe, adds a little sweetness and colour. Weigh it rather than using a spoon, and warm it slightly or weigh it straight into the water so it does not cling to the bowl. Not for babies under 12 months. |
| Extra virgin olive oil | Optional. A small amount, part of the app's recipe, keeps the crumb soft. Weigh it rather than using a spoon. |

## Kit

Essential:

- Digital scales (grams, 1 g resolution; 0.1 g is handy for salt)
- Large bowl or tub (a clear straight-sided tub makes it easy to judge the rise)
- Jar for the starter
- Something hot and heavy to bake in: a lidded cast-iron pot or casserole (a Dutch oven) works best at home. A pot of about 5 to 6 litres suits a regular loaf, and a large loaf is happier in about 6 to 7 litres. Check that the lid and knob are rated for {{ovenOn}}°C or more
- Wire rack

Strongly recommended:

- Bench scraper
- Banneton (proofing basket) or a bowl lined with a floured tea towel
- Rice flour for dusting the banneton (it does not stick)
- Sharp blade or lame for scoring
- Elastic bands or tape to mark the starting level on a jar or tub
- Thermometer (probe style, for dough and water; it can also check the baked loaf)
- Oven thermometer, because home ovens can sit well away from the number on the dial

Nice to have:

- Stand mixer (not required)
- Baking steel or stone
- Spray bottle
- Linen couche
- Parchment paper or a silicone sling for lifting the loaf into the pot
