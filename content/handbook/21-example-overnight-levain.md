A starter fed in the evening and left to rise overnight, a dough mixed first thing in the morning, and a loaf baked from the fridge on the third day. This example uses real numbers: 20 g of starter fed at 9 pm, in an overnight kitchen of about 18°C (65°F). The times are only an example, and the days are called Day 1, Day 2 and Day 3 because the bake can start on any evening. For a different evening, keep the same gaps and move the clock times to suit. The app's feed windows are for a warmer kitchen of about 21 to 22°C, where a {{standardFeed}} is ready in {{feedWindow 6}}. The estimates in this example only hold for a kitchen of about 18°C; a warmer or cooler kitchen changes them. Check every stage with the jar and the thermometer, not the clock.

> **Scaling the levain.** This example builds 260 g of levain, which is more than the [[recipe]] needs (150 g of ripe starter for a regular loaf, plus {{keepBack}} g to keep back). The ratio works at any size: a 1:6:6 feed is 1 part starter to 6 parts flour to 6 parts water, which is 13 parts in total, so multiply the starter weight by 13 to get the levain weight (20 g × 13 = 260 g). Build only as much as the recipe needs, plus a spoonful to keep going.

## Assumptions

- Starter: 20g, fed at 9pm on Day 1.
- Overnight kitchen temperature: about 18°C (65°F).
- Target levain: 260g, which the 1:6:6 ratio gives exactly.
- Autolyse: flour and water rest for 1 hour before the levain is added.

## Feed quantities (20g starter)

| Ratio | Starter | Flour | Water | Levain yield | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1:6:6 | 20g | 120g | 120g | 260g | Matches the 260g target |
| 1:7:7 | 20g | 140g | 140g | 300g | 40g more than the target |
| 1:8:8 | 20g | 160g | 160g | 340g | 80g more than the target |

## Option A: feed with room-temperature water (kitchen 18°C)

The levain starts at about 18°C, so it takes the whole night to peak. Times assume a 9pm feed on Day 1.

| Ratio | Estimated peak | Start autolyse | Add levain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1:6:6 | 8–9am on Day 2 | 7–8am | 8–9am |
| 1:7:7 | 10–11am on Day 2 | 9–10am | 10–11am |
| 1:8:8 | 12–1pm on Day 2 | 11am–12pm | 12–1pm |

The 1:6:6 option gives a sensible morning bake. The 1:7:7 and 1:8:8 options suit a later start.

## Option B: feed with 30°C water (kitchen 18°C)

Warm feed water raises the levain's starting temperature to about 23–24°C. It peaks much sooner, but the kitchen still cools it through the night, so the head start is shorter than the difference in water temperature alone would suggest.

| Ratio | Estimated peak | Start autolyse | Add levain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1:6:6 | ~3am on Day 2 | ~2am | ~3am |
| 1:7:7 | ~4am on Day 2 | ~3am | ~4am |
| 1:8:8 | ~5am on Day 2 | ~4am | ~5am |

For an 8am peak with warm water, feed the 1:6:6 at about 2am instead of 9pm. This keeps the bake on a similar schedule to Option A, with the levain peaking in the morning rather than the small hours.

## Why kitchen and water temperatures matter differently

- **Kitchen temperature** acts on the levain for the whole night. In an 18°C room, the yeast ferments slowly, so the levain takes 11–12 hours to peak with a 1:6:6 feed.
- **Water temperature** sets the starting point. The levain's temperature is roughly the average of its ingredients. For example, 120g flour at 18°C, 120g water at 30°C and 20g starter at 18°C gives a mix of about 23–24°C. Using 18°C water gives a mix of about 18°C.
- **Warm water gives a head start, not a lasting speed-up.** As the levain cools back towards the kitchen temperature overnight, the advantage shrinks. That is why 30°C water saves roughly 5–6 hours on a 1:6:6, not the full difference you'd see at a constant 24°C.
- **Dough temperature** controls the bulk, not the kitchen or the water. Measure the dough with a probe after mixing, then look it up in [[process#bulk|section 6, step 5]].

## Checklist

- **9pm, Day 1:** feed 20g starter with flour and water (room temperature or 30°C). Mark the jar level.
- **Early morning, Day 2:** check the jar. Start the autolyse 1 hour before the levain is due.
- **At peak:** the levain should be domed, bubbly and about double. Add it to the autolysed flour and water.
- **After mixing:** measure the dough temperature with a probe. Start the bulk clock and check the rise against the target in [[process#bulk|section 6, step 5]].
- **Before shaping (optional):** do the windowpane test. The dough should form a thin, translucent membrane.

## Signs the levain has peaked

- Domed top, with bubbles across the surface.
- Roughly double the feed volume (mark the jar with an elastic band).
- Slightly loose, airy texture, and a sweet-sour smell.
- If the top has started to fall back, it is past peak. Use it straight away or feed it again.

All times are estimates, based on the kitchen and water temperatures given. Your kitchen, starter and flour will change them. Use the jar and the thermometer to decide when to move on.
