Feed the starter in the evening and leave it to rise overnight. Mix the dough first thing the next morning, shape it in the afternoon, and bake from the fridge on the third day. The days are called Day 1, Day 2 and Day 3, because the bake can start on any evening.

## The feed

A feed is starter, flour and water mixed in a ratio such as 1:7:7 (1 part starter to 7 parts flour and 7 parts water). The more flour and water you give the starter, the longer it takes to be ready, so a bigger feed lets you wait longer before mixing. To make about the same amount each time, a bigger feed starts with less starter. Each of these makes enough for one regular loaf, with {{keepBack}} g left to keep as your next starter:

{{feedChoices 6 7 8}}

Overnight, a 1:7:7 is the one to use: fed at 9 pm, it is ready between 7 and 9 am, and goes in at about 8. A 1:6:6 would be ready an hour or so earlier, and a 1:8:8 an hour or so later. The times are for a kitchen of about 21 to 22°C. See [[starter#feeding-and-ratios|section 3.3]] for the others.

## If your kitchen is cooler

The starter takes longer to be ready in a cooler kitchen: a 1:7:7 in an 18°C kitchen needs a couple of hours more, so the dough is mixed later on Day 2. A dough that is cooler also bulks for longer. [[example-temperature|Section C6]] says how much.

In a cool kitchen, mix the feed with warm water (about 30°C). It starts warm, so the wait is shorter and you can feed later, closer to bedtime.

## Checklist

- Evening, Day 1: feed the starter with flour and water, using the amounts above. Mark the jar level with an elastic band.
- Morning, Day 2: check the jar. It should be domed, bubbly and about double. Start the autolyse an hour before you want to add it.
- After mixing: measure the dough temperature with a probe, and check the rise against the target in [[process#bulk|section 6, bulk fermentation]].

If the top of the starter has started to fall back, it is past its peak. Use it straight away or feed it again. All times are estimates: use the jar and the thermometer to decide when to move on.
