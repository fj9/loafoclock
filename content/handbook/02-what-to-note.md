Most sourdough problems come down to three questions (see [[troubleshooting|section 12]]): was the starter ripe when it was used, was the bulk fermentation long enough judged by the dough rather than the clock, and was the dough temperature what you thought it was. Noting down the facts below lets you, or Copilot, answer those questions afterwards.

| What to note | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Kitchen temperature | It sets the pace of every stage. |
| When you fed the starter, and the feed (for example 20 g starter, 100 g flour, 100 g water) | It decides when the starter peaks. |
| How the starter looked when you used it (risen? domed? bubbly?) | A ripe starter is the foundation of the whole bake. |
| Water temperature and dough temperature just after mixing | Dough temperature decides how long bulk fermentation takes. |
| Time bulk started and how much the dough has risen (mark the tub with a rubber band or tape) | Bulk is finished by how much the dough has risen, not by time alone. |
| Flour type and the weights in your recipe | Different flours and hydrations behave differently. |
| Final proof: room or fridge, how long, and the poke test result | Over-proofed and under-proofed dough bake differently. |
| Oven temperature, bake time, and how the crust and crumb turned out | Helps pin down what to change next time. |

**An example of a good question for Copilot**

> My loaf came out flat and gummy. My kitchen was about 20°C. I fed my starter at 8am with 20 g starter, 100 g flour and 100 g water and used it at 4pm. It had risen but wasn't domed. The dough was 22°C after mixing and I let it bulk for 5 hours without marking how far it rose. I shaped it, proofed it in the fridge overnight and baked it at 230°C for 40 minutes. What went wrong, and what should I change next time?
