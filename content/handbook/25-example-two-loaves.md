Make a double batch, shape two loaves, and put both in the fridge together. One is baked after 12 hours and the other after 36, so you have a loaf on Day 2 and another on Day 3, 24 hours apart. The second loaf spends longer in the cold, so it is the more sour of the two. That is useful if you want fresh bread on two different days, or one loaf to give away.

It is the same process as one loaf, with double the amounts and one extra step: after the bulk, divide the dough in two at the pre-shape.

## The plan

The plan is the same as the [[example-after-work|after work example]] as far as the fridge, then it splits in two. This is the first loaf, with 12 hours in the fridge.

**Day 1.** Feed the starter at {{time twoLoavesFirst feed}}, mix in the afternoon and do the four folds. When the bulk is done, at {{time twoLoavesFirst preShape}}, divide the dough in half, pre-shape both pieces, rest them {{benchRest}} minutes and shape them. Both go into the fridge at {{time twoLoavesFirst fridge}}.

**Day 2.** Bake the first loaf and leave the other in the fridge. Turn the oven on at {{time twoLoavesFirst ovenOn}}, bake at {{time twoLoavesFirst bake}}, and carve at {{time twoLoavesFirst carve}}.

## The second loaf

The second loaf stays in the fridge for 36 hours, and is baked in exactly the same way, 24 hours later:

- **Oven on:** {{at twoLoavesSecond ovenOn}}
- **Bake:** {{at twoLoavesSecond bake}}
- **Carve:** {{at twoLoavesSecond carve}}

The app allows up to {{fridgeMax}} hours in the fridge, so 36 is well inside it.

Keep the second loaf covered, and check it before you bake: a dough left in the fridge this long is looser and more sour. If it has spread or feels slack, shape it again, rest it for 30 minutes and bake it a little sooner.
