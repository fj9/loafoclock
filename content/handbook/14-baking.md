## Why steam matters

Steam keeps the crust soft and stretchy for the first part of baking so the loaf can expand fully ("oven spring"), and later helps produce a thin, shiny, crisp crust. In a lidded pot the steam comes from the dough itself, and the lid traps it.

## Oven temperatures

The oven temperatures in this handbook are for fan ovens. For a conventional oven, set it about 20°C higher, as long as your pot, lid and lid knob are rated for that (check the maker's maximum). Ovens vary a lot, so an oven thermometer helps, and colour matters more than the clock. If the loaf is browning faster than the table says, check it early. If it is still pale at the end, give it a few more minutes.

If your oven is gas, or cannot reach {{ovenOn}}°C in fan mode (about 260°C conventional), use its hottest setting and go by colour. Gas mark 9 is about 240°C conventional, or 220°C fan, so the bake will be a little slower. If the crust is still pale when the time is up, bake with the lid off for longer.

## Lidded pot or Dutch oven method (most reliable) {#dutch-oven steps=ovenOn,bake}

1. Put the empty pot with its lid in the oven and heat it to {{ovenOn}}°C. Allow {{preheat}} minutes.
2. Lift the shaped dough on parchment or a sling (straight from the fridge is fine). **Warning:** many supermarket baking papers stop at 220°C, which is below the {{ovenOn}}°C used here. Use a silicone sling, or baking paper rated for {{ovenOn}}°C or more (check the pack).
3. Score the top with a sharp blade at a shallow angle, about 0.5 to 1 cm deep.
4. Carefully lower the dough into the hot pot and put the lid on. The lid traps the steam from the dough, so you do not need to add any.
5. Bake with the lid on at {{ovenOn}}°C.
6. Lift off the lid, keeping your face back from the steam, then lower the oven to {{ovenLidOff}}°C and bake on until deep golden brown. How long depends on the loaf:

7. Optional: for the final 5 minutes, take the loaf out of the pot and set it directly on the oven rack for a crisper base.

## Open bake (steel/stone with steam)

1. Preheat a baking steel or stone for 45+ minutes at 240 to 250°C (fan oven).
2. Add steam: a tray of boiling water on a lower shelf. Be careful of steam burns and never pour cold water on glass.
3. Bake with steam for the first 15 to 20 minutes, remove the steam source, then continue to finish.

## No pot, steel or stone

- A lidded oven-safe casserole dish can stand in for a Dutch oven if its lid and knob are rated for {{ovenOn}}°C. Check the maker's limit, and do not use glass.
- Without a pot, bake on a heavy baking tray that has been heated in the oven first, with a tray of boiling water on the shelf below. Follow the open bake method above, using the hot tray in place of the steel or stone. The warning about baking paper in the pot method applies here too. Expect less oven spring and a paler crust, so bake until deep golden.

## Scoring

- Scoring controls where the loaf expands.
- Use a sharp blade or lame at a 30 to 45° angle for an "ear" (raised lip), or 90° for a flat cut.
- One confident, curved slash is better than hesitant sawing.
- Cold dough scores more cleanly.

## Doneness

- Colour first: deep golden-brown to dark mahogany crust; pale loaves taste flat.
- Hollow sound when tapped on the base.
- Optional: an internal temperature of 96 to 99°C, if you have a probe thermometer.

## Cooling {steps=out,carve}

Wait at least {{coolingLong}} minutes ({{coolingShort}} if you have no self-control; the app offers both). The crumb is still cooking from residual heat and moisture redistributes as it cools. The loaf will crackle as it cools. That is the crust singing, and it means the bake went well.
