## Flour types

| Flour | Effect |
|---|---|
| Strong white bread flour | High protein, strong gluten, chewy, open crumb |
| Plain/all-purpose white | Lower protein; softer, less rise |
| Wholemeal/whole wheat | More flavour and nutrients, absorbs more water, faster ferment, denser crumb |
| Rye | Very active ferment, sticky, weak gluten network, deep flavour; use as a portion (5–30%) rather than the whole |
| Spelt | Delicate gluten, nutty; ferments fast, can over-ferment easily |
| Einkorn / emmer | Ancient grains with weak gluten; handle gently and use less water |
| Semolina / durum | Gives a golden colour and sweet flavour in small amounts |
| Malted flour/barley malt | Adds flavour, colour and helps browning |

## Practical points

- Protein content matters more than the brand. Strong bread flour handles higher hydration.
- Freshly milled or stoneground flour is more active and absorbs more water.
- Wholemeal and rye speed up fermentation, so shorten times when adding them.
- Rye, spelt, einkorn and emmer all contain gluten, so none of them is gluten free.
- Gluten free: this guide is for wheat dough. Sourdough made with wheat is not gluten free, however long it ferments (see [[food-safety|section 15]]).
