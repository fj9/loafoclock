Sourdough's tang comes from two acids:

- Lactic acid: mild, creamy, yoghurt-like.
- Acetic acid: sharp, vinegary.

These are tendencies rather than rules, because starters and kitchens differ.

| To get a more sour loaf | To get a milder loaf |
|---|---|
| Longer fridge proof (24 hours or more) | Shorter or no fridge proof |
| Cooler dough temperature | Warmer dough (26–28°C) |
| Stiffer starter (lower hydration) | Wetter starter (higher hydration, up to 150%) |
| More wholemeal/rye | Mostly white flour |
| Use starter slightly past peak (slower and harder to judge, so try this once you know your starter) | Use starter at or just before peak |
| Less starter in the dough (longer ferment) | More starter, shorter ferment |

Salt and baking also affect flavour: well-baked, dark crust has deeper, caramelised flavours that balance acidity.
