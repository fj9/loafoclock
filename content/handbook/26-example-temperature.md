These examples use a dough at 24°C to 27°C and a kitchen of about 21 to 22°C. Yours will differ, and temperature moves the whole schedule. You do not need to redraw an example to see how:

- The dough temperature decides the bulk. The chart in [[process#bulk|section 6, bulk fermentation]] gives the time and the target rise for every dough temperature. For example, a 22°C dough needs {{bulk 22}}, and a warmer dough needs less. The examples use the longer end of each range, so add or take away the difference between the longer ends, and move every step after the bulk by the same amount. Bulk is counted from the moment the starter goes in.
- The kitchen temperature decides when the starter is ready. The feed windows in [[starter#feeding-and-ratios|section 3.3]] are for about 21 to 22°C. A cooler kitchen takes longer: a {{standardFeed}} at 18°C takes a couple of hours longer.
- Warm or cool water sets the dough temperature and starts the starter's feed warmer or cooler ([[levers#lever-2-dough-temperature-how-fast-bulk-goes|section 7, lever 2]]).

If a step would fall in the night, change the feed (a bigger feed is ready later) or the dough temperature, or let the app find a time that fits your day.
