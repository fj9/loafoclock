| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Dense, tight crumb with tiny holes | Underfermented bulk, weak starter, or underproofed | Longer bulk (judge by volume), stronger/ripe starter, warmer dough |
| Gummy, wet crumb | Cut too soon, underbaked, or overfermented | Cool fully, bake longer, check fermentation timing |
| Flat loaf that spreads | Overproofed, too wet, weak shaping, weak gluten | Shorter proof, lower hydration, tighter shaping, more folds early |
| Huge holes with dense bottom | Uneven fermentation, rough shaping trapping gas pockets | Be gentle but firm in shaping, deflate large bubbles at pre-shape |
| No ear / poor oven spring | Overproofed, shallow scoring, not enough steam, oven not hot | Score more deeply at an angle, check steam and heat, shorten proof |
| Pale crust | Bake too short or too cool, too little sugar from fermentation | Bake longer with the lid off, higher heat, add a little malt |
| Burnt bottom | Pot or stone too hot, thin base | Place a tray beneath the pot, preheat less, use a baking sheet under the pot |
| Too sour | Long fridge time, cool temperatures, starter past peak | Shorten cold retard, warmer dough, use ripe starter |
| Not sour enough | Short ferment, warm, high starter | Longer cold retard, cooler dough, some wholemeal/rye |
| Dough is sticky and won't hold shape | Overfermented or very high hydration | Reduce water, shorten bulk, use stronger flour |
| Dough tears when shaping | Underfermented or too stiff | Longer bulk, more hydration, rest before shaping |
| Dough sticks to the banneton | Not enough flour | Use rice flour, flour generously, or use a liner |
| Crust too thick and hard | Too long in the oven, too little steam | Shorten the bake, make sure the lid is on for the first stage |
| Crust blisters (small bubbles) | Cold retard; normal | This is a good sign, especially after fridge proof |
| Pale streaks or flour pockets | Poor mixing | Mix more thoroughly at the start |
| Dough doesn't rise at all | Dead or very weak starter, too cold | Test the starter, warm the dough, give more time |

## Diagnosing with the "three questions"

- **Was the starter ripe when I used it?** (Right feed ratio, right timing.)
- **Was bulk fermentation long enough, judged by dough rather than clock?**
- **Was my dough temperature what I thought it was?** (Most surprises come from a cooler or warmer kitchen than expected.)
