Bakers express every ingredient as a percentage of **total flour weight** (flour = 100%). This makes recipes easy to scale and compare.

| Ingredient | Typical % of flour |
|---|---|
| Flour | 100% |
| Water (hydration) | 60–85% |
| Salt | 1.8–2.2% (2% standard) |
| Starter | 10–35% (the app's recipe uses about {{starterPercent}}%) |

**Hydration** = water ÷ flour × 100.

| Hydration | Dough feel | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 60–65% | Stiff, easy to handle | Dense, tight crumb; bagels, some enriched loaves |
| 65–70% | Manageable | Good for beginners |
| 70–75% | Soft, a bit sticky | Open crumb with strong flour; the usual sweet spot |
| 75–85% | Slack, wet | Open, glossy crumb; harder to shape |

Accounting for the starter: a 100% hydration starter is half flour and half water. If you add 100 g starter, that is 50 g flour and 50 g water. Include those in your totals when calculating true hydration.

Starter and salt percentages use the flour you weigh into the bowl. For example, 150 g of starter on 450 g of flour is 33%. Hydration is different, because the starter holds flour too: 150 g of starter has 75 g of flour in it, so for hydration the flour counts as 525 g. That is why the real hydration can sit a point or two from the figure you choose.

More whole grain absorbs more water. If you swap in wholemeal, expect to add more water to get the same feel.
