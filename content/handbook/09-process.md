## Step 1: Prepare the starter (or levain) {steps=feed}

Feed it so that it will be ripe when you want to mix. Work backwards from the mixing time using the table in [[starter#feeding-and-ratios|section 3.3]]. For example, a {{standardFeed}} feed (the app's standard) is ready in {{feedWindow 6}}. The app starts from a starter kept in the fridge, fed straight from the jar.

A **levain** is simply a portion of starter built up in a separate jar for the bake, so the main starter jar stays untouched. Many bakers build a levain of just enough for the recipe plus a spoonful to keep going.

## Step 2: Autolyse (optional but recommended) {steps=autolyse}

Mix the flour and water only. Cover and rest for {{restMin}} to {{restMax}} minutes ({{restDefault}} is the usual).

- Flour fully hydrates and gluten starts to form without effort.
- The dough becomes smoother and easier to handle.
- Use the rest time to let the starter reach its peak.

The app adds the starter after the rest, then rests the dough again (a "fermentolyse", the next step). Salt goes in after that, because it tightens dough and slows enzyme action.

## Step 3: Add the starter, then the salt, honey and oil {#starter-and-salt steps=starterIn,salt}

- Add the active starter and mix until fully incorporated. Rest the dough for {{restMin}} to {{restMax}} minutes ({{restDefault}} is the usual). This is the **fermentolyse**, and it is when bulk fermentation starts, so note the time.
- Then add the salt, honey and olive oil (a splash of water helps dissolve the salt). Work them in until evenly spread, and rest the dough for {{saltRest}} minutes.
- Use wet hands to squeeze, fold and pinch the dough until fully combined. This takes 2-5 minutes by hand; a stand mixer takes a couple of minutes on low.
- The dough will look shaggy and rough. That is fine.

## Step 4: Build strength {steps=fold1,fold2,fold3,fold4}

You want gluten strong enough to hold gas but not overworked. There are several approaches:

| Method | How | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Stretch and folds | Wet hand, lift one side of the dough high, fold over the centre. Turn the bowl a quarter and repeat 4 times. Do 3-6 sets, spaced 20-45 minutes apart in the first half of bulk. | The most popular home method; gentle and low effort |
| Coil folds | Lift the centre of the dough and let it fold underneath itself in a coil. | Wetter doughs; very gentle |
| Slap and fold / kneading | Work the dough on the bench for 5-10 minutes. | Faster strength building; stiffer or enriched doughs |
| Stand mixer | A few minutes at low speed, then rest. | Large batches, wet doughs |
| No-knead | Long rest with few or no folds. | Very wet doughs and lazy mornings |

**In the app:** {{foldCount}} sets of stretch and folds, {{foldGap}} minutes apart, the first one {{saltRest}} minutes after the salt goes in. Bulky inclusions such as cheese, fruit or chocolate go in during the last fold.

A good rule: do the folds early in bulk while the dough is still slack, then leave it alone for the last half so the gas can build up undisturbed.

## Step 5: Bulk fermentation (the first rise) {#bulk steps=starterIn,preShape}

This is the longest and most important stage. It builds flavour, structure and gas.

**Bulk time by dough temperature.** Measure the dough with a probe just after mixing in the starter, then look up the closest temperature. Bulk is counted from the moment the starter goes in.

{{bulkTable}}

These times are for the app's recipe, which uses about {{starterPercent}}% starter. Less starter means longer; more starter means shorter. Above 27°C bulk is faster still, so check early.

**Judge by the dough, not the clock:**

- **Volume:** the chart gives the target rise for your dough temperature, from 50% for a warm dough to 75% for a cool one. Mark the start level on a straight-sided tub, or use a small straight-sided jar of dough (an aliquot jar).
- **Surface:** domed, slightly glossy, with bubbles visible at the edges or surface.
- **Edges:** the dough pulls away from the container cleanly.
- **Feel:** airy, jiggly like jelly, and more cohesive than at the start.
- **Wobble test:** gently shake the tub; if the whole mass jiggles, it is likely ready.

**Underfermented:** little rise, dense, tight, tears when shaped. Gives a tight, gummy crumb with small holes and often a pale crust.

**Overfermented:** very slack, sticky, collapses when shaped, smells sharply sour. Gives flat loaves, thin pale crust and gummy sticky crumb.

## Step 6: Pre-shape and bench rest {steps=preShape}

- Tip the dough gently onto a lightly floured (or unfloured) bench.
- Divide if making more than one loaf.
- Gather each piece into a loose round, using a scraper to drag it and build surface tension.
- Rest uncovered or covered for {{benchRest}} minutes. The dough relaxes so the final shape is easier.

## Step 7: Final shape {steps=finalShape}

Aim for a taut surface and a closed seam without tearing the skin.

**Boule (round):** pull the edges to the centre in a rough envelope, flip, and drag toward you on the bench to tighten.

**Batard (oval):** fold the top down, fold the sides in like a letter, roll up from the top, seal the seam.

Place seam-side up in a well-floured banneton (rice flour works well because it does not stick), or seam-side down in a lined bowl.

## Step 8: Final proof {steps=fridge}

Two main options:

| Option | How | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Room temperature proof | about 2 to 3 hours, covered, at about 22-25°C | Milder flavour; quicker; easy to over-proof |
| Cold retard (fridge proof) | {{fridgeMin}} to {{fridgeMax}} hours at about 3-5°C | More flavour, blistered crust, easier scoring, flexible timing |

The **poke test** is the classic readiness check. Flour a finger and press about 1 cm into the dough:

- Springs back immediately: underproofed, needs longer.
- Springs back slowly and leaves a slight dent: ready.
- Does not spring back and stays dented: very proofed, bake now.

Cold dough springs back more slowly in general, so judge it by volume and feel as well.

## Step 10: Cool {steps=out,carve}

Cool on a wire rack for **at least {{coolingShort}} minutes**. The app offers {{coolingShort}} or {{coolingLong}} minutes, with {{coolingLong}} as the default, and longer is better. The crumb is still setting and steaming inside; cutting early gives a gummy texture and compresses the loaf.

You should hear the crust crackle as it cools. This is called "singing".
